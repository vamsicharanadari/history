      Causes
        Nationalism
          Balkan tensions
          Slavic movements vs Austria-Hungary
        Alliances
          Triple Entente
          Triple Alliance
          Chain reaction mobilization
        Militarism
          Arms race (navy, artillery)
          War planning (Schlieffen Plan)
        Imperial Rivalry
          Colonial competition
        Political Fragility
          Declining empires (Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian)

      Immediate Trigger
        Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand
        Austria declares war on Serbia
        Alliance escalation

      How Powers Tried to Win
        Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman)
          Blitz strategy (Schlieffen Plan)
          U-boat submarine warfare
          Eastern front mobility
          Resource extraction from occupied lands
        Allies (UK, France, Russia, later USA)
          Trench warfare defense
          Naval blockade on Germany
          War of attrition
          Industrial mass production
          US entry boosts manpower & economy

      Where They Failed
        Central Powers Failures
          Schlieffen Plan failure → two-front war
          Resource shortages due to blockade
          Overextension
          US entry shifts balance
        Allied Weaknesses
          Massive casualties in trench warfare
          Slow progress (Somme, Verdun)
          Russia collapse (1917 Revolution)

      Outcome
        Germany defeated
        Empires collapse (German, Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, Russian)

    Interwar Transition (1918–1939)
      Treaty of Versailles
        War guilt on Germany
        Heavy reparations
        Territorial losses
      Economic Collapse
        Hyperinflation in Germany
        Great Depression (1929)
      Political Instability
        Weak democracies
        Rise of extremist ideologies
      Rise of Dictators
        Hitler (Germany)
        Mussolini (Italy)
        Militarists (Japan)
      Failed Peace Mechanisms
        Weak League of Nations
        Appeasement policy
      Social Psychology
        Humiliation (Germany)
        Revenge sentiment
        Fear of communism

    WWII (1939–1945)
      Causes
        Versailles resentment
        Economic crisis
        Expansionism
          Germany (Europe)
          Japan (Asia)
          Italy (Africa)
        Appeasement failure
        Weak international system

      Immediate Trigger
        Germany invades Poland (1939)
        Britain & France declare war

      How Powers Tried to Win
        Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, Japan)
          Blitzkrieg (fast mechanized warfare)
          Air superiority (Luftwaffe)
          U-boat warfare (Atlantic)
          Expansion for resources
          Surprise attacks (Pearl Harbor)
        Allies (UK, USSR, USA, others)
          Total war economy
          Strategic bombing campaigns
          Naval dominance
          Soviet attrition warfare
          D-Day invasion (multi-front attack)

      Where They Failed
        Axis Failures
          Overexpansion (Eastern Front, North Africa)
          Failed invasion of USSR
          Resource shortages (oil, materials)
          US industrial dominance
          Two-front war again
        Allied Weaknesses
          Early losses (France fall, Pearl Harbor)
          High casualties (Eastern Front)

      Outcome
        Axis defeat
        Germany divided
        Japan surrenders after atomic bombs

    Deeper Systemic Causes (Both Wars)
      Power Shifts
        Rising Germany vs old empires
      Economic Competition
        Industrial rivalry
      Ideologies
        Democracy vs Fascism vs Communism
      Technology
        Industrialized warfare
        Mechanization
      Human Factors
        Leadership miscalculations
        National pride
        Fear and insecurity
